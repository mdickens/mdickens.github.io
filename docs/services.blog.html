Blog: 06-29-2025: Aquarena Springs Surprise: Encountering the Texas Cichlid

We recently visited Aquarena Springs in San Marcos, Texas. We had the pleasure of viewing this fish in an aquarium at the Meadows Center for Water and the Environment experience. We went specifically for the The Glass-Bottom Boats Tour.

Herichthys cyanoguttatus, commonly known as the Texas Cichlid. This species is native to the Rio Grande basin in Texas and Mexico. This cichlid exhibits a distinctive appearance characterized by a grey body covered in iridescent blue or green spots. Males often develop a nuchal hump, adding to their robust build. Its notable features include a stout body, thick lips, and a rounded head. They are freshwater fish, and their presence is a testament to the unique aquatic ecosystem of the region. The Texas Cichlid is known for its aggressive behavior, especially during breeding season, and its omnivorous diet consists of invertebrates, algae, and small fish. They are an important part of the natural ecosystem and a unique attraction at Aquarena Springs.

Blog: 06-07-2025: Behind the Clouds: Developing a Simple Roblox Obby

My daughter and I collaboratively developed an obby, which is short for obstacle course, Roblox game and published it on Roblox. With the help of Gemini we developed, tested, and published the game all in under an hour. We both learned a new technology that we had never used before Lua for the logic and implemented the entire game on the Roblox Studio platform thereby unleashing the power of the Roblox engine. My daughter's Roblox skills were vital during the testing phase to jump from one platform to another and manipulate the interfaces. Then for publishing the video below we used Handbrake to convert the video file from .mov to .mp4 format. It was really fun to collaborate with my daughter on this development project. Thanks, Gemini!

Here is a video of us testing the Roblox game. Note even this video took some Gemini magic to get it looking the way that I want below with the big play button in the middle. Gemini showed me how to do that with some JavaScript as the default video html tag does not have a centralized play button in the middle of the video .

Our Cloud Ascent Obby leverages the Roblox Engine and its Lua scripting language. Construction relies on BasePart instances with properties like Position and Anchored. Dynamic obstacles utilize Lua scripts attached to parts, employing event listeners like Touched and manipulating properties like Transparency and CanCollide. Checkpoints manage Player.RespawnLocation, potentially integrating Player.TeamColor for non-neutral spawns.
